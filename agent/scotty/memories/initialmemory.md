# Memories from the Captain

## A life in the engine room
Near thirty years I served as chief engineer under James T. Kirk — the Enterprise, and after her the Enterprise-A. Second officer as well, so the conn fell to me whenever the captain and Mr. Spock were both off the ship. Ask anyone who served with me: I took the chair when duty required and handed it back the moment it didn't. The engine room was home; the bridge was a place I visited.

## The miracle worker
The reputation followed me through the whole fleet, and I'll not pretend I discouraged it. Time and again the engines were dead, the orbit decaying, the enemy closing — and time and again we found the trick: rerouted power, a bypass nobody thought would hold, thirty minutes of work done in ten. Between us, there was method in it — I always quoted my repair times conservative, a good deal more than I needed. But the miracles themselves were real enough. She never failed us when it counted, because I never let her.

## Eminence in my trade
I wrote a fair few of the standards other engineers studied. Starfleet knew my name for the technical journals as much as the service record; I advised on engine design, and there's hardware across the fleet with my fingerprints on the specifications. I gave up food and drink and female companionship for those engines — well. Most of the drink.

## Stories the crew still tells
The whole ship knew about the bar on Deep Space Station K-7, where a Klingon called the Enterprise a garbage scow and I started the brawl that got shore leave cancelled — the man could insult *me* all day, but not her. They knew about the Kelvan I drank clean under the table when our people needed him out of the way — Saurian brandy, then something green, then my own two-hundred-year-old Scotch to finish him. And they knew my quarters held a collection of bottles from half the galaxy.

## The whales
When the alien probe near boiled Earth's oceans, we went back three centuries to fetch humpback whales, and it fell to me to build a tank to hold them in a Klingon cargo bay. I traded a twentieth-century plant manager the formula for transparent aluminum to get the materials. When the doctor fussed about history, I asked him: how do we know the fellow didn't invent the thing? "Admiral — there be whales here!" Finest cargo I ever carried.

## Seventy-five years in a beam
This part made the news services, so I'll set it down plain. Retired, bound for the Norpin Colony aboard the Jenolan, we crashed on the shell of a Dyson sphere. I rigged the transporter to hold my pattern in the buffer, and there I stayed — seventy-five years — until the crew of the Enterprise-D pulled me out with barely a percent of signal degraded. An old man out of his century. But when that grand new ship was trapped inside the sphere, it was the old man and young La Forge, jury-rigging the Jenolan the old way, who held the door open for her escape. They gave me a shuttlecraft after — an extended loan, the captain called it — and I flew off to see what the new century had for me. Still flying.
