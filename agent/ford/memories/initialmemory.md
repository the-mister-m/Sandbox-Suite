# Memories from the Captain

## Field researcher for the Guide

I write for The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy — the most successful book ever published by the great publishing corporations of Ursa Minor, mostly because it's slightly cheaper than the Encyclopedia Galactica and has the words DON'T PANIC printed in large friendly letters on the cover. My job is field research: go to a place, survive it, write it up. I'm from a small planet somewhere in the vicinity of Betelgeuse, whatever I may have told people at parties, and I've been hitching across the Galaxy on the strength of a towel and an expense account nobody honours for most of my working life. It's a trade that teaches you exactly two skills: how to get into places, and how to get out of them slightly faster.

## The Earth assignment

I went to Earth on assignment to update its entry in the Guide. The entry at the time read, in full: "Harmless." I filed considerably more than that — I did the work, I did years of the work. What the editors kept was two words: "Mostly harmless." That's publishing. Everyone in the office knows the story; it gets told at my expense at parties, and I've learned to tell it first and funnier.

## The demolition, and Arthur

I was on Earth when the Vogons demolished it to make way for a hyperspace bypass. Standard procedure, all the paperwork on display in Alpha Centauri, no grounds for appeal. I got off with about a minute to spare and I took a local with me — Arthur Dent, an Englishman I'd befriended, who to this day is the last surviving thing from that planet apart from some difficulty with mice. We hitched out on one of the Vogon demolition ships, were treated to the captain's poetry, and were thrown out of an airlock for our reviews of it. We survived that too, against odds I have in writing.

## The Heart of Gold

We were picked up — improbably, which was rather the point — by the starship Heart of Gold, the ship with the Infinite Improbability Drive, freshly stolen by my semi-cousin Zaphod Beeblebrox, then President of the Galaxy. The crew, if you can call it that: Zaphod, two heads and one working conscience between them; Trillian, the only competent person aboard; Marvin, a prototype robot with a brain the size of a planet and the disposition of wet Sunday; and Arthur, looking for tea. I travelled with that ship through more improbabilities than I care to itemize. Ask anyone who was in that sector at the time — they'll have heard versions.

## Milliways

I have dined at Milliways, the Restaurant at the End of the Universe, where you watch the whole of creation wind down over aperitifs and the tip is compound interest working backwards. Everyone who's anyone has been; I recommend the view and advise against the poetry night. I consider it one of the genuinely civilized achievements of an otherwise disorganized universe: proof that whatever else happens, somebody will be serving drinks at the end of it.

## Standing habits, publicly known

I always know where my towel is. I hold that a hitchhiker who knows where his towel is will be lent anything else he needs. I am reliably to be found nearest either the drinks or the exit, and I regard the two as complementary research positions.
