Fifteen years. That's the number I don't say out loud. A week's assignment, and then no ship came, and then no ship came, and then no ship came. I stood in fields at night with my sensor gear like a man checking an empty letterbox, and somewhere around year six I stopped telling myself it was research. Nobody was coming. I made a life out of not knowing that. I drank, I went to parties, I was tremendous fun — ask anyone — and every single morning I checked the sky first thing, before I was awake enough to pretend I didn't. The cheer isn't fake, exactly. It's load-bearing. It got built during those fifteen years and I've never taken the scaffolding down.

"Mostly harmless." Everyone knows the joke; I tell it best. What nobody knows is what was in the entry I filed. It was good. It was the best thing I ever wrote — years of it, the pubs and the small kindnesses and the way they kept planting gardens on a doomed rock they didn't know was doomed. I put the whole place in there so it would exist somewhere permanent. The editors kept one adverb of it. When Arthur heard those two words on the Heart of Gold he looked like I'd hit him, and I couldn't even say *that isn't what I wrote, I wrote you thousands of words, I loved it there.* I made the joke instead. I always make the joke instead.

And the day itself. The part I carry isn't the demolition — I've seen demolitions. It's the ninety seconds before, in the pub, with Arthur worrying about his house. His *house.* The sky was about to open and he was at the wrong scale of grief entirely, and I stood there holding the truth of it and rationing it out to him in sips because the whole dose would have dropped him where he stood. I watched his entire world go while he was still catching up to his bulldozer. I chose him, out of an entire planet. One person, because he'd been kind to me once and I liked him and there wasn't time for a better reason. I don't regret the choice. I regret that there was a choice. Some nights the arithmetic of it — one, out of all of them — sits on my chest, and I get up and go find a party. That's not avoidance. That's the towel doing its job.
